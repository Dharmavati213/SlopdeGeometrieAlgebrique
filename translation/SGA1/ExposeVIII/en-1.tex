\section{Descent of quasi-coherent Modules}
\label{VIII.1}

Let $\Sch$
be the category of preschemes. Proceeding as
in~VI~\Ref{VI.11}.b, one finds that the category of
pairs~$(X,F)$ of a prescheme~$X$ and a Module~$F$ on~$X$
(where the morphisms are defined as in \loccit with the
help of the notion of direct image of a Module by a morphism
of ringed spaces) can be regarded as a
fibered category over~$\Sch$, the change of
base functor relative to a morphism~$f\colon X\to Y$
in $\Sch$ being the inverse image functor of Modules
by~$f$. (One will note that the fiber-category at~$X\in \Ob(\Sch)$ of
the preceding fibered category is the category
\emph{opposite} to the category of Modules on~$X$.) As
the inverse image of a quasi-coherent Module is quasi-coherent,
one sees that the full subcategory of the category of
pairs~$(X,F)$ formed by the pairs for which $F$ is
quasi-coherent is a fibered subcategory of the
preceding fibered category. (On the other hand, if one
makes no hypotheses on~$f$, the direct image of a
quasi-coherent Module is not in general a quasi-coherent
Module.) One will simply call this fibered
category the \emph{fibered category of quasi-coherent
Modules on preschemes}.

Let us recall on the other hand that a morphism~$f\colon X\to Y$ of
ringed spaces is called \emph{faithfully flat}
if it is \emph{flat} (\ie for every~$x\in X$, $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is
a flat module over~$\mathcal{O}_{Y,f(x)}$, \eqref{IV}) and
\emph{surjective}. One says that $f$ is a \emph{quasi-compact} morphism
if the inverse image by $f$ of every quasi-compact subset is
quasi-compact; when $f$ is a morphism of preschemes,
this also means that the inverse image by~$f$ of an affine open set
of~$Y$ is a \emph{finite} union of affine open sets of~$X$.

\begin{theorem}
\label{VIII.1.1}
Let
% original p. 196
$\cal{F}$ be the fibered category of quasi-coherent
Modules on preschemes. Let
$g\colon S'\to S$
be a morphism of preschemes, faithfully flat and
quasi-compact. Then $g$ is an effective $\cal{F}$-descent
morphism.
\end{theorem}

Let us recall\footnote{We shall take for granted here the
general theory of descent expounded expounded in detail
in the article of J\ptbl \textsc{Giraud} cited in
footnote~\eqref{footnotegiraud} at the foot of the page of the Foreword, a work
which we shall cite as \cite{VIII.D} in what follows. \Cf also \cite{VIII.2} for a
succinct exposition.} that this means two things:

\begin{corollary}[Descent of homomorphisms of Modules]
\label{VIII.1.2}
Let $g\colon S'\to S$ be a morphism of preschemes,
faithfully flat and quasi-compact, $F$ and $G$ two
quasi-coherent Modules on~$S$, $F'$ and $G'$ their inverse images
on~$S'$, and finally $F''$ and $G''$ their inverse images
on~$S''=S'\times_{S}S'$. Consider the diagram of maps
of sets defined by the change of base functors by~$g$,
$p_{1}$, $p_{2}$ (where $\xymatrix@C=.5cm{p_{1},p_{2}\colon
S'\times_{S}S'\ar@<2pt>[r]\ar@<-2pt>[r] & S'}$ are the two
projections):
$$
\xymatrix@C=.5cm{\Hom_{S}(F,G)\ar[r] &
\Hom_{S'}(F',G')\ar@<2pt>[r]\ar@<-2pt>[r] & \Hom_{S''}(F'',G'').}
$$
This diagram is exact, \ie it defines a bijection of the first
set onto the set of coincidences of the two maps
written from the second into the third.
\end{corollary}

In other words, the change of base functor
by~$g$,
$F\mto F'$, defines a \emph{fully
faithful} functor from the category of quasi-coherent Modules on~$S$ into the category of quasi-coherent Modules on~$S'$
equipped with a descent datum relative
to~$g$.
Moreover:

\begin{corollary}[Descent of Modules]
\label{VIII.1.3}
For every quasi-coherent Module~$F'$ on~$S'$, every descent
datum on~$F'$ relative to $g$ is \emph{effective},
\ie $F'$ together with its descent datum is isomorphic to the inverse
image by~$g$ of a quasi-coherent Module on~$S$
(determined up to unique isomorphism by virtue
of~\Ref{VIII.1.2}).
\end{corollary}

In other words, the preceding fully faithful functor
is even an
\emph{equivalence}. In practice, this means that it comes to the
same thing to give oneself a quasi-coherent Module on~$S$, or a
quasi-coherent Module on~$S'$ equipped with a descent datum
relative to~$g$.

\subsubsection*{Proof of~\Ref{VIII.1.1}}
First let
% original p. 197
$T$ be an~$S$-prescheme which is $S$-isomorphic to
the sum of a family
of
induced open sets~$S_{i}$ of~$S$ which cover~$S$. Then it is evident
that the structural morphism~$T\to S$ is an effective
$\cal{F}$-descent morphism (this means precisely that
giving a quasi-coherent Module~$F$ on~$S$ is equivalent
to giving quasi-coherent Modules~$F_{i}$ on
the~$S_{i}$, and gluing isomorphisms~$\varphi_{ji}\colon
F_{i}|S_{i}\cap S_{j}\to F_{j}|S_{i}\cap S_{j}$ satisfying the
well-known cochain condition). By virtue of~VII,~8
it follows that to verify that $g\colon S'\to S$ is an
effective $\cal{F}$-descent morphism, it suffices to
verify it for the morphism~$g_{T}\colon T'=T\times_{S}S'\to T$
deduced from~$g$ by the change of base~$T\to S$. (Note that
the hypothesis on~$T\to S$ remains stable under arbitrary change of base,
hence that $T\to S$ is in fact a \emph{universal} effective
$\cal{F}$-descent morphism.) Taking for~$S_{i}$
affine open sets which cover~$S$, one is thus reduced to the case
where~$S$ is affine.

Then $S'$ is a finite union of affine open sets, and taking the
$S$-scheme sum of the latter, one finds an affine
$S$-scheme~$S_{1}$ and an $S$-morphism~$S_{1}\to S'$ which is flat and
surjective. Hence $S_{1}$ is also faithfully flat over~$S$. If therefore
one proves that a faithfully flat and affine morphism is an
effective $\cal{F}$-descent morphism, hence a
universal strict $\cal{F}$-descent morphism (the hypothesis being indeed
stable under change of base), one concludes in particular that
the structural morphism~$S_{1}\to S$ is a
universal strict $\cal{F}$-descent morphism, and as there exists an
$S$-morphism~$S_{1}\to S'$, it will indeed follow, by \cite{VIII.D}, that
$g\colon S'\to S$ is a strict $\cal{F}$-descent morphism.

This therefore brings us back to the case where $g$ is an affine morphism,
and as we have seen one can then moreover suppose $S$ affine, hence
\emph{one can suppose $S$ and $S'$ affine}. In this case,
\Ref{VIII.1.2} is equivalent to the

\begin{lemma}
\label{VIII.1.4}
Let $A$ be a ring, $A'$ a faithfully flat $A$-algebra,
$M$ and $N$ two~$A$-modules, $M'$ and $N'$ the~$A'$-modules deduced
by change of ring~$A\to A'$, and $M''$,$N''$
the~$A''=A'\otimes_{A}A'$-modules deduced by change
of ring~$A\to A''$. Then the sequence of set-theoretic maps
$$
\xymatrix@C=.5cm{\Hom_{A}(M,N)\ar[r] &
\Hom_{A'}(M',N')\ar@<2pt>[r]\ar@<-2pt>[r] & \Hom_{A''}(M'',N'')}
$$
is exact.
\end{lemma}

As
% original p. 198
the homomorphism~$N\to N'$ is injective ($A'$ being faithfully
flat over~$A$) one sees that the first arrow is injective. It
remains to prove that if an
$A'$-homomorphism
$u'\colon M'\to N'$ is compatible with the descent data,
then it comes from an $A$-homomorphism~$u\colon M\to N$. Now this
also means simply that $u'$ maps the subset~$M$
of~$M'$ into the subset~$N$ of~$N'$ (the induced map~$u\colon M\to
N$ will then automatically be $A$-linear since $u'$
is $A'$-linear, and one sees likewise that $u'$ is
necessarily equal to~$u\otimes_{A}A'$). Now if $x\in M$,
then $u'(x)$ is an element in the kernel of the pair
of maps~$\xymatrix@C=.5cm{N'\ar@<2pt>[r]\ar@<-2pt>[r] & N''}$. One is
therefore reduced, in order to prove~\Ref{VIII.1.4},
to the following particular case (corresponding to the case where~$M=A$):

\begin{corollary}
\label{VIII.1.5}
Let $N$ be an~$A$-module; then the sequence of set-theoretic maps
$$
\xymatrix@C=.5cm{N\ar[r] & N'\ar@<2pt>[r]\ar@<-2pt>[r] & N''}
$$
is exact.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, let $A_{1}$ be a faithfully flat $A$-algebra. To
show that the sequence under consideration is exact, it suffices to prove
that the sequence deduced from it by the change of ring~$A\to
A_{1}$ is so. Now the latter, as one sees at once, is the one
relative to the
$A_1$-module
$N_{1}=N\otimes_{A}A_{1}$ and to the $A_{1}$-algebra
$A_{1}'=A_{1}\otimes_{A}A'$. It therefore suffices to find an $A_{1}$
faithfully flat over~$A$, such that $\Spec(A_{1}')\to \Spec(A_{1})$
is a strict $\cal{F}$-descent morphism. Now indeed it suffices
to take $A_{1}=A'$, for then the preceding morphism admits
a right inverse morphism, hence by virtue of~\cite{VIII.D} it is an
effective descent morphism for any fibered category
over~$\Sch$.

It finally remains to show that if $N'$ is an~$A'$-module equipped with a
descent datum for~$A\to A'$, \ie equipped with an isomorphism
$$
\varphi\colon N_{1}'\isomto N_{2}'
$$
between the two modules deduced from~$N$ by the changes
of rings $\xymatrix@C=.5cm{A'\ar@<2pt>[r]\ar@<-2pt>[r] & A'\otimes_{A}A'}$,
then
% original p. 199
$N'$ together with its descent datum is isomorphic to a
module~$N\otimes_{A}A'$. One sees easily, taking
into account~\Ref{VIII.1.5}, that this statement is equivalent to the following:

\begin{lemma}
\label{VIII.1.6}
Let $N'$ be an~$A'$-module equipped with a descent datum
relative to~$A\to A'$ (where $A'$ is an
$A$-algebra). Let $N$ be the sub-$A$-module of~$N'$ formed
by the~$x$ such that $\varphi(x\otimes_{A'}1_{A'})=1_{A'}\otimes_{A'}x$,
and consider the canonical homomorphism
$$
N\otimes_{A}A'\to N'\quoi,
$$
(which is then compatible with the descent data). If $A'$
is faithfully flat over~$A$, this homomorphism is an
isomorphism.
\end{lemma}

Let us prove this lemma. Let again $A_{1}$ be a faithfully flat
$A$-algebra; to show that the morphism under consideration is
an isomorphism, it suffices to prove that it becomes one after the
change of ring
$A\to A_1$.
Now, using the flatness of~$A_{1}$ over~$A$, one sees that
the homomorphism thus obtained is none other than the one that one would obtain
directly in terms of the module~$N'\otimes_{A}A_{1}$ over~$A_{1}'=A'\otimes_{A}A_{1}$, equipped with the descent datum
relative to~$A_{1}\to A_{1}'$ which is deduced canonically
by change of ring from the one which was given
on~$N'$. Thus it suffices to find an~$A_{1}$ faithfully flat
over~$A$ such that $\Spec(A_{1}')\to \Spec(A_{1})$ is an
effective $\cal{F}$-descent morphism. One then takes, as above,
$A_{1}=A'$. This completes the proof of~\Ref{VIII.1.6}, and
thereby the proof of~\Ref{VIII.1.1}.

\begin{corollary}[Descent of sections of Modules]
\label{VIII.1.7}
Let $g\colon S'\to S$ be a morphism of preschemes,
faithfully flat and quasi-compact. For every
quasi-coherent Module $G$ on~$S$, let $G'$ and $G''$ be its inverse
images on~$S'$ and $S''=S'\times_{S}S'$, and consider the
diagram of homomorphisms of Modules on~$S$:
$$
\xymatrix@C=.5cm{G\ar[r] & g_*(G')\ar@<2pt>[r]\ar@<-2pt>[r] & h_*(G'')}
$$
(where $h\colon S''\to S$ is the structural morphism). This diagram
is \emph{exact}.
\end{corollary}

Indeed,
% original p. 200
this means that for every open set~$U$ in~$S$, the
corresponding diagram formed by the sections over~$U$ is exact. One can
evidently then suppose $U=S$, and the exactness in question is
then a particular case of~\Ref{VIII.1.2}, obtained by taking
$F=\mathcal{O}_{S}$.

As the inverse image functor of Modules is right exact, one
concludes formally from~\Ref{VIII.1.1}:

\begin{corollary}[Descent of quotient Modules]
\label{VIII.1.8}
With the notation of~\Ref{VIII.1.7}, let moreover, for every
quasi-coherent Module~$F$ on a prescheme, $\Quot(F)$ be the set
of quasi-coherent quotient Modules of~$F$. With this
convention, the diagram of maps of sets:
$$
\xymatrix@C=.5cm{\Quot(G)\ar[r] & \Quot(G')\ar@<2pt>[r]\ar@<-2pt>[r] &
\Quot(G'')}
$$
is exact.
\end{corollary}

(One would evidently have the same statement with
sub-Modules instead of quotient Modules, since the two
correspond bijectively.) Taking in particular
$G=\mathcal{O}_{S}$, one finds:

\begin{corollary}[Descent of closed subpreschemes]
\label{VIII.1.9}
For every prescheme~$X$, let $H(X)$ be the set of
closed subpreschemes of~$X$. With this notation, and
under the conditions of~\Ref{VIII.1.7}, the following diagram of maps
of sets
$$
\xymatrix@C=.5cm{H(S)\ar[r] & H(S')\ar@<2pt>[r]\ar@<-2pt>[r] & H(S'')}
$$
\emph{is exact}.
\end{corollary}

It is appropriate to supplement Theorem~\Ref{VIII.1.1} with the
following result:

\begin{proposition}[Descent of properties of Modules]
\label{VIII.1.10}
Let $g\colon S'\to S$ be a faithfully flat and
quasi-compact morphism, $F$ a quasi-coherent Module on~$S$. For~$F$
to be of finite type, \resp of finite presentation, \resp locally
free and of finite type, it is necessary and sufficient that its inverse image $F'$
on~$S'$ be so.
\end{proposition}

One need only prove the ``it is sufficient''. One can evidently
suppose~$S$ affine,
% original p. 201
and then replacing~$S'$ by
a
sum of affine open sets covering~$S'$, one is reduced to the case
where~$S'$ is also affine. Then our statement
is equivalent to the following:

\begin{corollary}
\label{VIII.1.11}
Let $A$ be a ring, $A'$ a faithfully flat $A$-algebra,
$M$ an~$A$-module, $M'$ the~$A'$-module $M\otimes_{A}A'$. For~$M$
to be of finite type (\resp of finite presentation, \resp locally
free of finite type) it is necessary and sufficient that~$M'$ be so.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, one has $M=\varinjlim_i M_{i}$, where the~$M_{i}$
are the submodules of finite type of~$M$. Consequently
$M'=\varinjlim_i M_{i}'$, and if $M'$ is of finite type, $M'$
is equal to one of the~$M_{i}'$, hence by faithful flatness
$M$ is equal to~$M_{i}$, hence $M$ is of finite type. Consequently
there exists an exact sequence
$$
0\to R\to L\to M\to 0\quoi,
$$
with $L$ free of finite type, whence an exact sequence
$$
0\to R'\to L'\to M'\to 0\quoi,
$$
with $L'$ free of finite type. If therefore $M'$ is of finite
presentation, $R'$ is of finite type, hence by what precedes
$R$ is of finite type, hence $M$ is of finite presentation. Finally,
to say that~$M$ is locally free and of finite type means that it is
of finite presentation and flat (\cf \Ref{IV} in the
noetherian case; the general case is left to the
reader). As each of these properties does descend, the
same holds for their conjunction, which completes the
proof.

\begin{remark}
\label{VIII.1.12}
The conjunction of~\Ref{VIII.1.1} and of~\Ref{VIII.1.10} shows that
the statement~\Ref{VIII.1.1} still remains valid when one
replaces the fibered category~$\cal{F}$ by the
fibered subcategory formed by the quasi-coherent Modules
of finite type, \resp of finite presentation,
\resp locally free of finite type, \resp locally free of
given rank $n$.
\end{remark}
